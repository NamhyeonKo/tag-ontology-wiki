# Concept tags

Use `tags:` for reusable concept entities a note directly explains: its title concept and the most specific concepts covered in its headings, definitions, or comparisons. Keep classification such as `type` and `category` in their fields, not in tags. Write tag examples in backticks in this manual; do not add Obsidian `tags:` frontmatter to it.

- Use lowercase letters and hyphens; tags are flat (no `/` hierarchy). Keep one compound concept whole, and use separate tags for distinct concepts.
- Tag concepts that can be reused across many notes: fields, taxonomy nodes, named techniques, algorithms, attacks, systems, standards, tools, products, and vendors. Do not tag qualities, metrics, section names, specific API identifiers, passing mentions, or terms unique to one paper.
- Keep a broader tag when the note explains that concept directly too; omit it when it is only background.
- Three to ten tags are recommended; use at most 15 (20 for an overview covering at least three numbered sections). Consider splitting a topic-heavy note before removing useful tags.
- The tag dictionary is the source of truth for aliases, definitions, and broader/related concepts. Look up an existing tag before proposing a new one; get approval before registering or applying new tags. The user chooses tags applied to notes.
